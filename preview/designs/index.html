Atrium — one layout, ten palettes

One direction, the one that survived: Atrium — the 04 inspector panel married to the 05 cinematic cover. Full-bleed splash art, the skill, its class icons and the character running it, the three numbers, and the combinations stacked top-down as full-width cards — filterable by Large / Medium / Small — with a single glass panel on the right carrying the selected combination, the notables ranking, the unique jewels (switchable off), the routes and the totals. Nothing about the layout changes below: only the palette does.

Ten dark palettes, none of them brown or green. The first five: Crimson (the original, de-pinked, with a gold highlight), Violet with a pale yellow, Azure with a pale red, Cyan, and Noir — black and white with a single gold. Then five more, each carrying more than one colour: Neon, magenta chrome with cyan values and an amber action; Ember, an orange chrome with ice-blue modifiers; Rose Gold, rose chrome, gold notables and periwinkle modifiers; Honey, where the gold becomes the chrome itself; and Steel, a matte desaturated blue-grey. Colour only — the layout, the sizes and the type are identical in all ten. In every one, the notables keep one colour and the cluster modifiers another, so the two categories never blur together.

Every frame carries the whole cluster-jewel surface: share, average and peak DPS, the three combinations with their notables and cluster modifiers, socketing (size · passives · iLvl floor · price), the unique cluster jewels (Voices 21, Megalomaniac 34), the most-used notables, the three crafting routes with tries and cost, and the all-builds totals.

The frames are alive: hover anything that looks like a control — buttons, rows, tiles, tabs, the panel itself — and it lifts, glows or moves. Click a skill gem to change the build, click a combination to change the crafting target, click LARGE / MEDIUM / SMALL to filter the list, and flick the switch beside Unique cluster jewels to hide Voices and Megalomaniac. Hover a combination and the cluster jewel tooltip flies out — name, base type, item level, socket, modifiers and notables, exactly as the app shows it. The last tab in the bar is Settings: click it and the whole screen becomes a settings page — league, snapshot, auto-refresh, the cluster-jewel switches, density and number format — with Cancel or Save taking you back to the list. 1:1 opens a frame at full size.
